## D1. Pre-index imaging window widened from 1 year to 2 years (2026-07-21)
- **Original:** protocol section 8 selects the eligible contralateral study 1 to 365 days before index.
- **Deviation:** the LOCKED primary uses 1 to 730 days (2 years); 365 and 1,095 days are retained as sensitivities.
- **Rationale:** the re-gate grid showed the 365-day window cannot reach the 500-event floor under any strategy. Two years is a defensible baseline horizon for slowly-progressive osteoarthritis and clears the floor.
- **Status:** author-approved (Decision E, 2026-07-21).

## D2. Side-recovery arm adopted as the PRIMARY cohort (2026-07-21)
- **Original:** protocol section 6 requires the first qualifying CPT 27447 to have exactly one interpretable RT or LT modifier (strict, coded laterality only).
- **Deviation:** the primary cohort is `recovery_any` = strict coded index UNION a side-recovered index, where a blank-modifier earliest 27447 has its side recovered from concordant, no-conflict, same-day-or-earlier signals. This inferred the index side for 49.3% of the primary cohort.
- **Rationale:** 61% of CPT-27447 records carry no laterality modifier; the strict coded cohort tops out at 420 events even with lifetime imaging and cannot be powered. Only the index side is recovered; predictor imaging and the coded outcome remain gold-standard.
- **Mitigation / condition:** the protocol section-7 laterality QA audit (>=200 patients) must validate the recovered index sides before the cohort is locked for training; `recovery_confirmed` (>=2 signals) and the strict arm are pre-specified fallbacks. The strict coded cohort is retained as the high-specificity sensitivity analysis.
- **Status:** author-approved (Decision E, 2026-07-21).

## D3. Infection exclusion uses the high-specificity definition (2026-07-21)
- **Original:** protocol section 6 excludes knee joint infection or osteomyelitis in the 365 days before index.
- **Deviation / choice:** high-specificity definition = curated `knee_osteomyelitis` only; the broader `knee_osteomyelitis OR joint_infection` is reported as a sensitivity. Both remove a negligible number of patients (6 vs 10 of the strict candidates).
- **Status:** author-approved (Decision B, 2026-07-21).

## D4. Observation-through-day-90 defined by last-observed date (2026-07-21)
- **Choice:** a patient is observed through day 90 if the maximum date across CPT, ICD, pain, and image records exceeds index + 90 days; a stricter record-in-window variant is documented.
- **Status:** author-approved (Decision C, 2026-07-21).

## D5. Same-day blank companion lines treated as same-side billing artifacts (2026-07-21)
- **Choice:** when a patient's earliest 27447 date carries a single RT or LT plus one or more same-day blank-modifier 27447 lines (2,604 strict patients), the blank lines are treated as same-side billing artifacts of the one procedure, not a separate same-day contralateral TKA. No patient has RT and LT on the same earliest date, and the downstream gates would catch a genuine same-day bilateral.
- **Status:** author-approved (Decision A, 2026-07-21).

## D6. Event vs censoring horizon (documented, faithful to protocol)
- The primary event must occur within 5 years of index (protocol section 9), while censoring is at 5 years after the day-90 landmark (protocol section 10). These differ by 90 days and are handled explicitly; because events are capped at index + 1,826 days, the wider censoring horizon never miscounts an event.

## D7. Reproducibility environment (Python 3.14 instead of 3.12)

- **Deviation:** the scientific stack (duckdb, pandas, pyarrow, lifelines, matplotlib, numpy) is installed only in the active system interpreter (Python 3.14.0); pyenv 3.12.8 lacks it. `.python-version` is `system`; `requirements.txt` pins exact versions.

## D8. Five-year evaluation horizon clamped to day 1825 (2026-07-24)
- **Original:** protocol sections 9 and 24 specify outcome horizons of 1, 2, and 5 years; 5 years from the day-90 landmark is day 1826 (5 x 365.25).
- **Deviation:** every horizon-specific metric (IPCW cumulative/dynamic AUROC, calibration slope and intercept, horizon risk) is evaluated at day **1825**, one day earlier. Horizons of 1 and 2 years are unaffected (days 365 and 730).
- **Rationale:** censoring is administrative at exactly day 1826, so **no patient is observed event-free beyond it**. The cumulative/dynamic control set is `T > t`; at t = 1826 it is empty and the censoring survival function G(1826) = 0, which makes the inverse-probability-of-censoring weights infinite and the estimator undefined. Day 1825 (4.997 y) is the last day strictly inside observed follow-up. The clamp is computed, logged and reported, never silent.
- **Implementation:** one shared helper, `src.model_clinical.clamp_horizon_days`, is imported by `src/sample_size_riley.py`, which previously used day 1826 and therefore disagreed with M0 by a day. `outputs/tables/sample_size_riley.csv` now carries a `horizon_days` column and the module asserts its grid equals the one frozen in `derived-data/cohort/m0_clinical_model.json`.
- **Impact:** the 5-year metrics describe day 1825 rather than day 1826 and must be labelled as such in the manuscript. The 2-year co-primary horizon is unaffected.
- **Status:** analyst decision, documented; no author sign-off sought because it is forced by the data rather than chosen.

## D9. Aliased missing indicator removed from the model column set (2026-07-24)
- **Original:** protocol sections 12 and 21 pre-specify explicit missingness indicators for the incompletely observed predictors (contralateral inferred KLG and pre-index pain score).
- **Deviation:** `pain_score_max_missing` is still computed and written to `derived-data/cohort/features_clinical.parquet`, but is **excluded from `model_columns`**, i.e. it does not enter the M0 design matrix.
- **Superseded in part by D14 (2026-07-24):** this entry originally added "`klg_contra_missing` is unaffected and remains a predictor". That is no longer true and was itself the defect D14 corrects — `klg_contra` and `klg_contra_missing` are **M1** columns (protocol Table 7) and are out of M0 entirely. `klg_contra_missing` is still written to the parquet, still defines M1's eligible subset, and still drives the complete-case sensitivity filter.
- **Rationale:** `pain_score_max` is aggregated only over rows with a knee-pain flag, because protocol Table 5 defines the predictor as a *knee* pain score and widening it would admit shoulder and back scores. The exact arithmetic consequence in this cohort is that a pain score exists for **precisely** the 3,441 of 3,709 patients with a pre-index knee-pain record, so `knee_pain_any_imp + pain_score_max_missing = 1` on every row. A Cox partial likelihood has no intercept, so that pair spans a direction the likelihood cannot see: ridge split one shared effect into two equal and opposite coefficients (+0.3567 / -0.3567) and the reported `knee_pain_any` hazard ratio of 1.43 was **half** the identified effect, with a z of 0.62 that belonged to an unidentified direction. Predictions were never affected; the printed coefficient was.
- **Impact:** the design matrix goes from 15 columns to 14. `knee_pain_any_imp` now carries the whole effect: **HR 1.97 (z +2.79, p 0.005)**, replacing the previously printed HR 1.43 (z 0.62, p 0.53). Knee pain therefore moves from "no detectable signal" to a real one, which is a reportable change of substance. Headline discrimination barely moves (validation C-index 0.6680 to 0.6677). The alias is re-verified from the data on every run, so a future cohort in which the two stop coinciding will automatically re-admit the indicator.
- **Alternative rejected:** redefining `pain_score_max` over all pre-index pain rows would also remove the alias, but at the cost of protocol fidelity (non-knee pain scores entering a knee pain predictor). Bookkeeping must not drive the predictor definition.
- **Status:** analyst decision, documented; affects a reported hazard ratio, so it is flagged for author review before the manuscript quotes the pain effect.

## D10. Riley candidate-parameter count is the IDENTIFIED count, not the column count (2026-07-24)
- **Original:** protocol section 16 requires the Riley minimum development sample to be computed "with the final number of candidate parameters".
- **Deviation:** P is taken from `identified_parameters` in `derived-data/cohort/m0_clinical_model.json`, computed by rank as `rank([X | 1]) - 1`, giving **P = 13** against 14 design columns. The previous calculation used P = 15.
- **Rationale:** Riley's criteria describe how many parameters the likelihood must estimate. Two directions in the design are not estimable: the aliased pain indicator (removed under D9) and patsy's `cr()` age basis, which is a **partition of unity** (`age_rcs1 + age_rcs2 + age_rcs3 = 1` on every row) and therefore spans the constant that an intercept-free Cox model cannot identify. A pairwise correlation scan cannot see a three-column relation, which is why identifiability is now decided by rank.
- **Impact:** the primary requirement falls from 1,502 to **1,301** patients. The previous P = 15 was **conservative** — it demanded more development data than the model needs — so every "development sample is sufficient" verdict already recorded still holds a fortiori. The age spline contributes 2 effective degrees of freedom, not 3, and no individual `age_rcs*` hazard ratio may be reported; the coefficient table flags them as level-unidentified.
- **Updated by D14/D15 (2026-07-24):** the M0 design changed again — `klg_contra_imp` and `klg_contra_missing` out, `days_to_index_imp` in — so M0 now has **13 design columns and P = 12 identified**, and the primary Riley requirement falls again from 1,301 to **1,201** patients. The method is unchanged; only the column set moved. Fewer parameters lower every requirement, so this direction is conservative with respect to the verdicts already recorded. Development n = 2,968 with 427 events remains **SUFFICIENT**.
- **Status:** analyst decision, documented.

## D12. Test-set precision: protocol floor met, analyst-adopted half-width target not met (2026-07-24)
- **Original:** protocol section 16 asks for "acceptable precision for 5-year AUROC and calibration slope" and states one numeric floor: "a practical preliminary floor is 500 total primary events and 100 test events". It specifies **no numeric precision target**.
- **Position:** the protocol floor **IS met** — 533 total primary events (>= 500) and 106 test events (>= 100). The +/-0.05 AUROC and +/-0.20 calibration-slope 95% CI half-widths in `config/feasibility.yaml` are **analyst-adopted** and are stricter than anything the protocol requires. A previous version of `outputs/sample_size.md` called them "the pre-specified targets" and juxtaposed the shortfall with a section-16 citation, which misattributed an analyst choice to the protocol; the config and the report now label them explicitly.
- **Finding:** by simulation, 741 test patients give a 5-year AUROC half-width of 0.065 and a calibration-slope half-width of 0.291 at an assumed true AUROC of 0.70. Meeting both adopted targets would need about 1,570 test patients. The simulation assumes a normal linear predictor and censoring independent of risk, so these are **lower bounds** on real-world uncertainty.
- **Proposed protocol amendment (not yet enacted; requires author decision before the test set is unsealed):** (a) re-specify the primary test-set estimand as a **paired** comparison against the clinical baseline M0 — a difference in AUROC and in the index of prediction accuracy — rather than an absolute AUROC level, since paired differences share patients and are far more precisely estimated; and (c) pre-specify the achievable precision honestly in the protocol and the manuscript, stating that the study is powered to demonstrate discrimination better than chance and better than the clinical baseline, not to certify a point estimate. Increasing the test fraction (option b) is explicitly **not** recommended: it requires re-drawing the LOCKED splits (protocol section 17), which is an investigator decision, and no module in this pipeline changes them.
- **Status:** flagged for author decision. Nothing has been re-split; the locked splits are untouched.

## D13. Protocol section 24 landmark sensitivity (day 30 / day 180) deferred (2026-07-24)
- **Original:** protocol section 24 lists "landmarks at day 30, day 90, and day 180" among the sensitivity analyses.
- **Deviation:** only the pre-specified primary landmark, **day 90**, is analysed. The day-30 and day-180 landmarks are **not run**; `outputs/tables/m0_metrics.csv` records the row `sens_landmark_30_90_180` as `not_run` with the reason.
- **Rationale:** the landmark is not an evaluation parameter that can be varied downstream; it defines the cohort. Moving it changes **eligibility** (who is event-free and still observed at the landmark), the time origin of `time_from_landmark`, the censoring distribution, and therefore the event counts and the locked patient splits. Producing it means re-running the Phase-1 follow-up/outcome pipeline (`src/followup.py`, `src/outcomes.py`, `src/assemble_cohort.py`) at each landmark and rebuilding `final_cohort.parquet`, `patient_splits.parquet` and `features_clinical.parquet` — i.e. overwriting locked files. The module refuses to touch them.
- **What it would take:** add landmark-parameterised output paths so each alternative cohort is written to its own directory; re-derive splits per landmark under the same seed; re-run M0 on each; report the three side by side. Estimated as a self-contained task, not a variation of the current run.
- **Status:** deferred, documented. To be completed before submission or explicitly declared not done, with this rationale, in the manuscript's limitations.

## D14. Inferred KLG removed from M0; M1 added (CORRECTION, 2026-07-24)
- **Original:** protocol Table 7 defines the model ladder as **M0 = "Age, sex, comorbidities, pain, image-to-index interval"** and **M1 = "M0 plus inferred KLG"**. Protocol Table 6 lists the dataset-inferred contralateral KLG as a **"Secondary comparator only"**. Protocol Table 8 makes the primary estimand *M4 multimodal versus M0 clinical-only, IPCW cumulative/dynamic AUROC at 5 years*.
- **The defect:** an earlier revision of `config/feasibility.yaml` placed `klg_contra` in `features_clinical.primary_predictors`, so the fitted M0 design contained `klg_contra_imp` **and** `klg_contra_missing`. KLG was M0's **dominant coefficient** (HR 1.63, z 9.2; `klg_contra_missing` HR 2.06, z 3.2). The clinical comparator the image models must beat therefore already contained a radiograph-derived severity grade, which makes the primary estimand a comparison of imaging against imaging and biases the measured incremental value of imaging **toward zero**. Five tracked artefacts already stated that inferred KLG is a secondary comparator only (`outputs/protocol_deviations.md`, `outputs/data_quality_report.md`, `outputs/protocol_to_column_mapping.csv`, `outputs/feasibility_stage1_checkpoint.md`, `src/inventory.py`), so the fitted model contradicted the project's own record. **M1 did not exist anywhere** — no module, notebook or report defined it — leaving protocol Secondary objective 2 unimplemented.
- **Correction:** `klg_contra` is out of `primary_predictors` and into a new `features_clinical.m1_predictors` (= M0 + `klg_contra`, `m1_requires_klg_eligible_subset: true`). `klg_contra`, `klg_contra_imp` and `klg_contra_missing` remain in `features_clinical.parquet` as M1 / evaluation columns. The frozen imputation JSON now carries both `model_columns` (M0, 11) and `m1_model_columns` (M1, 12). `src/model_clinical.py` fits M0 on the corrected set and then fits **M1 on the KLG-eligible subset only** (`klg_contra` observed: 3,566 of 3,709 cohort patients; train 2,500 / val 359 / test 707, with 351 / 52 / 98 events). **KLG is never median-imputed for M1** — imputing a severity grade for the 143 patients with no eligible bilateral frontal would fabricate the very measurement M1 exists to price. Rank-based identifiability is computed and persisted for both designs. The test split stays sealed in both.
- **M0 before vs after** (validation, n = 371, 54 events):

| M0 metric | before (KLG in M0) | after (protocol Table 7 M0) |
|---|---|---|
| design columns / identified parameters | 14 / 13 | **13 / 12** |
| chosen ridge penalizer | 0.01 | **1.0** (at the grid edge — see D23) |
| Harrell C-index (val) | 0.6677 | **0.6086** |
| IPCW C/D AUROC, 1 y (day 365) | 0.7139 | **0.6408** |
| IPCW C/D AUROC, 2 y (day 730) | 0.6762 | **0.6244** |
| IPCW C/D AUROC, 5 y (day 1825) | 0.7254 | **0.7004** |
| dominant coefficient | `klg_contra_imp` (HR 1.63, z 9.2); `klg_contra_missing` (HR 2.06, z 3.2) | none: at penalizer 1.0 every M0 hazard ratio is within 0.08 of 1, the largest being `knee_pain_any_imp` (HR 1.08, z 1.02) |

- **What inferred KLG actually adds** (M1, on the 359 KLG-eligible validation patients / 52 events, paired patient-level bootstrap, 500 replicates; both models refitted on the same eligible training rows so the *only* difference is the KLG column): Harrell C 0.5919 -> **0.6643**, difference **+0.0724 (95% CI -0.0015 to +0.1372, p = 0.064)**; IPCW AUROC at 1 y **+0.0819** (-0.016 to 0.167), at 2 y **+0.0618** (-0.034 to 0.135), at 5 y **+0.0300** (-0.050 to 0.100). In the M1 fit, KLG carries **HR 1.72 per grade (z +9.51)**. So the grade is a strong single predictor whose incremental contribution to discrimination is real but not separable from noise at 52 validation events — and it is now measured where the protocol puts it, as a secondary comparator, instead of being hidden inside the primary comparator.
- **Impact on the primary estimand:** M4-versus-M0 is now a comparison against routine clinical variables, as protocol Table 8 intends. M0's absolute performance is lower, which makes the imaging increment easier to demonstrate; that is not a reason to prefer this specification, it is a consequence of removing a predictor the protocol never put there. The manuscript must report M0's corrected numbers and must not quote the earlier ones.
- **Status:** correction, documented. No author sign-off sought: the protocol tables are unambiguous and the previous state contradicted them.

## D20. Comorbidity lookback is ALL pre-index history, not 5 years (2026-07-24) — FLAGGED FOR AUTHOR DECISION
- **Original:** protocol Table 6 defines the comorbidity predictor as "Any curated autoimmune, diabetes, hypertension, obesity, nicotine, or lower-extremity trauma flag **in prior 5 years**", and adds that the "exact lookback [is] fixed before test access".
- **Implemented:** `config/feasibility.yaml` sets `features_clinical.comorbidity_lookback_days: null`, i.e. **every** ICD row strictly before `index_date`, with no lower bound. Every patient contributes at least one pre-index diagnosis row, so no comorbidity 0 is unobserved; a longer lookback can only increase prevalence.
- **Why it was not silently changed:** the six comorbidity flags are chronic-disease markers, and a 5-year window would code a patient diagnosed with diabetes 6 years before index as non-diabetic. Both readings are defensible and the choice materially changes six of the eleven M0 predictors, so it is an author decision, not an analyst one.

## D26. The 32-patient batch is assembled as 8 accumulation steps of 4, and the DataLoader runs 0 workers (2026-07-26)
- **Original:** `config/feasibility.yaml` sets `model_image.local.grad_accum_steps: 1` and `model_image.local.num_workers: 4`, so the 32-patient batch would be one forward/backward pass of 32 patients loaded by 4 spawned worker processes.
- **Deviation:** local training is invoked as `--grad-accum 8 --num-workers 0`, i.e. the same 32-patient optimisation batch assembled from **8 micro-batches of 4 patients**, with data loaded in the main process.
- **What did NOT change:** the optimisation batch is still 32 patients, the loss, optimizer, schedule, augmentation, seeds, horizons and the 512 x 512 crop are all untouched. **Nothing was downscaled.** Protocol section 13 fixes the crop at 512^2, and `notebooks/train_colab.ipynb` cell 34 states the preference for gradient accumulation over downscaling; this is that preference exercised.
- **Rationale (measured, not assumed — plan step A3):** ConvNeXt-Tiny holds roughly 0.5 GB of fp32 backward activations per 512^2 crop, and because batching is per *patient* while crops per patient vary (mean 1.64, max 5), a micro-batch of 8 patients can carry up to 23 crops, about 11.5 GB, on a 24 GB machine. It intermittently thrashes. Measured `m4_fusion` step times on this M4 Pro: accumulation 1 did not complete 23 steps in 11 minutes (> 25 s/step); accumulation 2, 47.8 s/step; accumulation 4 with 4 workers, 2.67 s/step; accumulation 4 with 0 workers, 1.91 s/step; **accumulation 8 with 0 workers, 0.50 s/step**; accumulation 16, 0.28 s/step but no better per epoch. Micro-batch 4 caps the worst case at 12 crops (about 6 GB) and sustains the machine's ceiling of roughly 13 crops/s.
- **Why 0 workers rather than 4:** `persistent_workers` is off and macOS spawns rather than forks, so the worker pool is rebuilt every epoch. That cost recurs; it does not amortise. Removing it saves 7 to 8 s per epoch, cuts the `m4_fusion` validation pass from 209 s to 10 s and the clinical epoch from 38 s to 16 s. Data loading is nowhere near the bottleneck at 13 crops/s. Augmentation still varies per epoch at 0 workers because `PatientViewDataset.__getitem__` XORs `self.epoch` into the per-sample RNG seed for exactly this case.
- **Why this is recorded rather than written into the config:** both values are inputs to `TrainSettings.training_contract()` and therefore to the training-contract hash, which is `d61f0d617a697fe4` under these flags (against `f781e47085e6c486` for the config defaults). `train_one_seed` **refuses** a checkpoint written under a different hash rather than silently resuming across a changed contract, so the flags are load-bearing and must be identical for every invocation of both stages. They are hardware-specific tuning, not a scientific choice, and the config keeps the machine-independent specification.
- **Honest note on cost:** the measured runtime is **worse than the plan's extrapolation**. Plan step A3 estimated 45 to 65 min per seed per image arm and 24 to 30 h for the full 7-arm ladder. The measurement gives 89 min per seed at 16 epochs and 223 min at 40, i.e. **Stage 1 15.5 to 38.7 h, Stage 2 15.4 to 38.4 h, full ladder 30.8 to 77.1 h** depending on whether early stopping bites near epoch 16 (best epoch about 8 plus patience 8) or the seeds run the full 40. `max_epochs: 40` and `patience: 8` were **not** reduced to save time: they are pre-specified in protocol sections 14 and 15, and shortening them after seeing a runtime measurement would be a post-hoc change to the training contract.
- **Status:** operational tuning, documented. No scientific parameter was altered and the test split was not read.

## D29. Decision-curve analysis run at manuscript stage: pre-specified in kind, post-hoc in timing (2026-07-30)
- **Original:** protocol section 18 lists "exploratory decision-curve analysis over pre-specified 5-year risk thresholds" under clinical utility and cites Vickers, so the *analysis* is pre-specified. The **numeric thresholds** it calls pre-specified appear **nowhere**: not in the protocol, not in any config file, not in any module. There is no grid, no bounds and no plotting window anywhere in the pre-specification.
- **This closes D18**, which recorded on 2026-07-24 that no net-benefit computation was produced by any module or notebook, and deferred the analysis "to be produced at manuscript stage or declared not done". It has now been produced at manuscript stage, and that is exactly why the sequencing has to be stated rather than implied.
- **The sequencing, plainly.** The single permitted read of the locked test split was performed **first** (D31). The estimator, the threshold grid, the arm set and the figure were specified **after** it. The analysis is therefore **pre-specified in kind and post-hoc in timing**, and no reader should have to infer that from a date stamp. What did **not** change is the models: the fitted weights, the ensemble rule, the recalibration and the frozen training-contract hash `4b862b5ecb947314` are the same objects the sealed read scored, and `src.eval_models.assert_sealed_read_is_recorded` fails the render if any of them moves. What was added is a second **reading** of predictions that already existed, not a second fitting.
- **Mitigation 1: no test quantity entered the choice of thresholds.** The grid and the plotted window are anchored to the **development** 5-year Kaplan-Meier risk, **0.2002** (train + val, 2,968 patients, 427 events), and **never** to the test split's 0.2004. The two agree to four decimal places, and that is the point: the development anchor was available while the split was still sealed, so choosing it costs nothing and there is no version of this analysis in which a test number selected the range it is then evaluated over.
- **Mitigation 2: every threshold is reported.** `config/feasibility.yaml` sets `threshold_min_pct: 1` and `threshold_max_pct: 35`, and `outputs/tables/test_net_benefit.csv` carries all **35** thresholds for all **4** arms, 140 rows, with nothing suppressed and nothing trimmed. Five rows are flagged `sparse` (all `m0`, at p_t >= 0.31) and stay in the table carrying that flag. Figure 4 plots 0.02 to 0.30 and the full grid sits behind it, so a shopped threshold has nowhere to hide: whatever point a reader wants to check is already printed.
- **Mitigation 3: the intervals are described as what they are.** The bands are **pointwise** percentile intervals from the same 2,000-replicate patient-level bootstrap draw the rest of the evaluation uses, with **no multiplicity adjustment across thresholds**. The 35 flagged sets are nested, so the grid is 35 views of one curve rather than 35 hypotheses; the P value printed beside a threshold is not a member of a family and must not be read as one.
- **What this costs, stated plainly.** The section-18 utility analysis **cannot** be presented as a pre-specified test. Every threshold-specific claim (net benefit exceeding treat-all from about p_t = 0.12, exceeding the clinical model only from about p_t = 0.18) is an **exploratory** reading of an unbiased set of predictions. That is a real and useful thing to have; it is not the same thing as a pre-registered result, and the manuscript must not let the two blur.
- **Status:** analyst decision, documented. The reason it is documented at this length is that the alternative was to publish a decision curve whose threshold grid no reader could date.

## D30. Net benefit estimated by Kaplan-Meier within the flagged set, not by per-patient IPCW (2026-07-30)
- **Original:** protocol section 18 names decision-curve analysis and cites Vickers but specifies **no estimator**. The textbook net-benefit formula assumes complete follow-up, which this cohort does not have: **473 of 741 test patients (63.8%) are censored before day 1825** and only 162 (21.9%) are followed past it. The naive event fraction, 14.3%, understates the 5-year risk of 20.0% by **5.7 percentage points**, which is exactly where the treat-all line crosses zero. The estimator is therefore not a detail; it decides where the reference line sits.
- **Choice:** at each threshold the flagged set's 5-year event risk is estimated by **Kaplan-Meier at day 1825 within that flagged set**, so the true-positive and false-positive terms come from one fit, no censored patient is dropped and none is counted twice. An **inverse-probability-of-censoring-weighted** curve is computed on the same flagged sets and reported beside it as `net_benefit_ipcw`, as a **point-estimate sensitivity analysis only**. It deliberately carries no interval: it shares the primary estimator's bootstrap draw, so a second band would invite a reader to compare two intervals that are not independent.
- **Measured, and this is why both curves are printed.** The **absolute level** of net benefit is estimator-dependent at this sample size. Over the plotted window the two estimators differ by up to **0.016 on test** (arm `m0` at p_t = 0.20: 0.0396 against 0.0557, i.e. the gap is **41% of that arm's own estimate**) and by up to **0.028 on validation** (arm `m2_frontal` at p_t = 0.26). No absolute net-benefit value in this paper should be read as if the estimator were settled.
- **Measured, and this is why the paper leans on contrasts.** The **differences between arms** are far more stable than the levels. Across the **90** threshold-by-contrast points at or below p_t = 0.30 (three arms against the `m0` reference, 30 thresholds each), the IPCW contrast falls inside the primary estimator's 95% bootstrap interval at **89 of 90**. The single exception is `m2_frontal` at p_t = 0.01, where the IPCW difference is +0.000279 against an interval of +0.000363 to +0.002267: outside by about 8e-05, at the lowest threshold on the grid, and of the same sign. And wherever the primary estimator's interval **excludes zero**, the IPCW contrast has the **same sign at every one of those points**. The manuscript's clinical-utility claims are therefore stated as contrasts, with the absolute curve presented as the shape of the argument rather than as a calibrated quantity.
- **Status:** analyst decision, documented. Both curves live in `outputs/tables/{split}_net_benefit.csv` and neither is derived from the other.

## D31. The single permitted read of the locked test split was performed (2026-07-30)
- **Original:** protocol sections 12 and 17 permit exactly **one** scripted read of the locked test split, once the model, ensemble rule, thresholds and analysis script are frozen; protocol Table 8 defines the primary estimand (M4 versus M0, IPCW cumulative/dynamic AUROC at 5 years) on that split. D25 declared on 2026-07-26 that every Phase-2 number was validation-only and that the seal was intact.
- **What happened:** the read was performed. `src/score_test.py` scored the frozen ladder on the locked split, **741 patients with 106 events** (740 of them with crops, 1,216 crops), and the event is recorded in `derived-data/cohort/test_scoring.json` as `sealed_read: "PERFORMED. This is the single permitted read of the locked test split."` beside `training_contract_hash: "4b862b5ecb947314"`. **This supersedes D25.**
- **The gate, which is stronger than the flags beside it.** `src.eval_models.assert_sealed_read_is_recorded` runs at render time whenever the reported split is the sealed one. It requires that the record exists, that `sealed_read` begins `PERFORMED`, and that the `training_contract_hash` it carries **still equals** `derived-data/cohort/train_arms.json`'s. A model retrained, retuned or re-ensembled after the read therefore **cannot** be reported as an out-of-sample result: the render fails instead. `forbid_test_split` only stops a module reading test rows; it cannot detect a model that changed after they were read, which is the failure mode a sealed protocol exists to prevent and which nothing in this pipeline previously caught. The three `forbid_test_split: true` flags are **untouched** and still guard `model_clinical`, the local trainer and the validation evaluator.
- **What the read buys.** These are the only estimates in the study that took no part in fitting, hyperparameter choice, checkpoint selection or recalibration, so **they are unbiased out of sample**. The corollary, which D25 anticipated, now bites in the other direction: each image arm's checkpoint was chosen on validation negative log-likelihood and the recalibration was fitted on the same 371 patients the slope is then measured on, so a validation metric is optimistic **by construction** and is no longer reportable as performance.
- **The convergence gate changes meaning on this split, and the change is deliberate.** `severe_overfit` **no longer disqualifies** an arm when the reported split is the sealed one; `did_not_converge` still disqualifies on every split, because a constant predictor is constant everywhere. The `severe_overfit` flag exists because a validation metric computed at a validation-selected epoch is **circular**. A test estimate took no part in that selection, so it cannot be inflated by it, and it is precisely the number that reveals whether the overfitting cost anything. This is load-bearing rather than theoretical: **four arms carry the flag on the test convergence table, including the primary `m4_fusion`** (validation NLL ending 0.182 above its own minimum), so under the validation rule the primary contrast itself would have been blanked. The flag is still computed, still printed and still in `outputs/tables/test_convergence.csv`; it stops **suppressing**, it does not stop being **reported**.
- **The preconditions that were NOT met, recorded rather than buried.** `model_image.test.unlock_requires` lists six conditions. Model, ensemble rule and analysis script were frozen. The decision-curve **thresholds were not** (D29). **"Crop QA signed off" and "laterality QA audit passed" were not met either:** `outputs/crop_qa_checklist.md` still carries an **empty sign-off block**, with no reviewer name, no date and a blank `Result`, and `outputs/tables/laterality_audit_summary.csv` and `outputs/tables/outcome_audit_summary.csv` still report **sampling only**, with no adjudication, no raw agreement and no Cohen kappa. **D22 is unchanged and remains open.** The read was performed regardless, on the same author instruction D28 records: the study is not registered and the author is not bound by the pre-specified protocol. The consequence is that the D2 recovered-index-side cohort, 49.3% of the primary cohort, is **still unvalidated by a human reviewer**, and every test-split number in the manuscript rests on that unvalidated index side. This belongs in the manuscript's limitations, not only here.
- **Status:** author decision, taken and implemented. The read is a completed and recorded event, not a switch that can be flipped again: a second scored read of this split would violate the same protocol sections this entry cites.

## D32. Table 1 reports the test split's baseline characteristics, which required reading the sealed FEATURE rows (2026-07-30)
- **Original:** Table 1 crossed split with 5-year event status and printed four groups, **none of them the test split**. That answers "how do cases differ from non-cases", a question about the outcome that the models exist to answer properly, and it answers it with the outcome already spent.
- **Change:** Table 1 now puts **development beside test**, because the question a reader of a locked-split prediction paper actually has is whether the splits are comparable, and that needs them side by side. Producing it means materialising the **741 test rows of the clinical feature table**. That is a read of sealed rows, and it is recorded here as one.
- **Why this is a separate matter from D31, and why neither covers the other.** The sealed **scoring** read is the one protocol sections 12 and 17 ration, and it is **unchanged**: one scripted pass over the frozen ladder, recorded in `test_scoring.json`. A baseline-characteristics table reads predictors and follow-up time, produces no model output and can change no estimate. But it is still sealed data on a page, and a register that recorded only the scoring read would understate what has been looked at.
- **What constrains it.** One rule, `src.make_manuscript.readable_splits`, decides which splits' rows a render may hold: a validation render gets the development splits **and nothing else**, and only a sealed render gets the sealed rows, and only after `assert_sealed_read_is_recorded` has passed. Table 1's missingness column is computed over exactly those splits, so it can never be a percentage of patients the render did not read. `src/manuscript_figures.py` resolves the same question with the same expression and is tested against it, because a split one render module may read and the other may not is a contradiction rather than a configuration. The three `forbid_test_split: true` flags are untouched.
- **What this also fixed.** The previous state asserted, on **both** splits, that "the test split was never read: the reader takes a split predicate that excludes it" while a **validation** render was in fact materialising all 741 sealed rows for Table 1. So the document made a leakage-control claim about itself that was false in the one direction nobody was checking. A `--split val` render now reads **no sealed row at all**, and that is asserted rather than asserted-about.
- **Status:** correction and scope declaration, documented.

- The augmented outcome's radiographic confirmation is incomplete near the horizon (image coverage ends earlier than CPT coverage).
- Inferred KLG is available only for weight-bearing bilateral frontal non-arthroplasty views (~18% of images) and serves only as a secondary comparator. **Consistency check (2026-07-24):** this statement is now true of the fitted models as well as of the documentation.
