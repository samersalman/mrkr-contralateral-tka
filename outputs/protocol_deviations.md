## D1. Pre-index imaging window widened from 1 year to 2 years (2026-07-21)
- **Original:** protocol section 8 selects the eligible contralateral study 1 to 365 days before index.
- **Deviation:** the LOCKED primary uses 1 to 730 days (2 years); 365 and 1,095 days are retained as sensitivities.
- **Rationale:** the re-gate grid showed the 365-day window cannot reach the 500-event floor under any strategy. Two years is a defensible baseline horizon for slowly-progressive osteoarthritis and clears the floor.
- **Status:** author-approved (Decision E, 2026-07-21).

## D2. Side-recovery arm adopted as the PRIMARY cohort (2026-07-21)
- **Original:** protocol section 6 requires the first qualifying CPT 27447 to have exactly one interpretable RT or LT modifier (strict, coded laterality only).
- **Deviation:** the primary cohort is `recovery_any` = strict coded index UNION a side-recovered index, where a blank-modifier earliest 27447 has its side recovered from concordant, no-conflict, same-day-or-earlier signals. This inferred the index side for 49.3% of the primary cohort.
- **MACHINE-READ — do not reword the clause "This inferred the index side for *N*% of the primary cohort" in the bullet above.** `src.make_manuscript.manual_review_clause` scrapes it with the regex `inferred the index side for\s+\*{0,2}([\d.]+)%` and prints the capture inside a Discussion limitation; `tests/test_make_manuscript.py` asserts the round trip. Inserting words between "for" and the percentage, or moving the percentage ahead of it, drops the figure out of the manuscript without an error.
- **Rationale:** 61% of CPT-27447 records carry no laterality modifier; the strict coded cohort tops out at 420 events even with lifetime imaging and cannot be powered. Only the index side is recovered; predictor imaging and the coded outcome remain gold-standard.
- **Mitigation / condition:** the protocol section-7 laterality QA audit (>=200 patients) must validate the recovered index sides before the cohort is locked for training; `recovery_confirmed` (>=2 signals) and the strict arm are pre-specified fallbacks. The strict coded cohort is retained as the high-specificity sensitivity analysis.
- **Status:** author-approved (Decision E, 2026-07-21).

## D8. Five-year evaluation horizon clamped to day 1825 (2026-07-24)
- **Original:** protocol sections 9 and 24 specify outcome horizons of 1, 2, and 5 years; 5 years from the day-90 landmark is day 1826 (5 x 365.25).
- **Deviation:** every horizon-specific metric (IPCW cumulative/dynamic AUROC, calibration slope and intercept, horizon risk) is evaluated at day **1825**, one day earlier. Horizons of 1 and 2 years are unaffected (days 365 and 730).
- **Rationale:** censoring is administrative at exactly day 1826, so **no patient is observed event-free beyond it**. The cumulative/dynamic control set is `T > t`; at t = 1826 it is empty and the censoring survival function G(1826) = 0, which makes the inverse-probability-of-censoring weights infinite and the estimator undefined. Day 1825 (4.997 y) is the last day strictly inside observed follow-up. The clamp is computed, logged and reported, never silent.
- **Implementation:** one shared helper, `src.model_clinical.clamp_horizon_days`, is imported by `src/sample_size_riley.py`, which previously used day 1826 and therefore disagreed with M0 by a day. `outputs/tables/sample_size_riley.csv` now carries a `horizon_days` column and the module asserts its grid equals the one frozen in `derived-data/cohort/m0_clinical_model.json`.
- **Impact:** the 5-year metrics describe day 1825 rather than day 1826 and must be labelled as such in the manuscript. The 2-year co-primary horizon is unaffected.
- **Status:** analyst decision, documented; no author sign-off sought because it is forced by the data rather than chosen.

## D9. Aliased missing indicator removed from the model column set (2026-07-24)
- **Original:** protocol sections 12 and 21 pre-specify explicit missingness indicators for the incompletely observed predictors (contralateral inferred KLG and pre-index pain score).
- **Deviation:** `pain_score_max_missing` is still computed and written to `derived-data/cohort/features_clinical.parquet`, but is **excluded from `model_columns`**, i.e. it does not enter the M0 design matrix.
- **Superseded in part by D14 (2026-07-24):** this entry originally added "`klg_contra_missing` is unaffected and remains a predictor". That is no longer true and was itself the defect D14 corrects — `klg_contra` and `klg_contra_missing` are **M1** columns (protocol Table 7) and are out of M0 entirely. `klg_contra_missing` is still written to the parquet, still defines M1's eligible subset, and still drives the complete-case sensitivity filter.
- **Rationale:** `pain_score_max` is aggregated only over rows with a knee-pain flag, because protocol Table 5 defines the predictor as a *knee* pain score and widening it would admit shoulder and back scores. The exact arithmetic consequence in this cohort is that a pain score exists for **precisely** the 3,441 of 3,709 patients with a pre-index knee-pain record, so `knee_pain_any_imp + pain_score_max_missing = 1` on every row. A Cox partial likelihood has no intercept, so that pair spans a direction the likelihood cannot see: ridge split one shared effect into two equal and opposite coefficients (+0.3567 / -0.3567) and the reported `knee_pain_any` hazard ratio of 1.43 was **half** the identified effect, with a z of 0.62 that belonged to an unidentified direction. Predictions were never affected; the printed coefficient was.
- **Impact:** the design matrix goes from 15 columns to 14. `knee_pain_any_imp` now carries the whole effect: **HR 1.97 (z +2.79, p 0.005)**, replacing the previously printed HR 1.43 (z 0.62, p 0.53). Knee pain therefore moves from "no detectable signal" to a real one, which is a reportable change of substance. Headline discrimination barely moves (validation C-index 0.6680 to 0.6677). The alias is re-verified from the data on every run, so a future cohort in which the two stop coinciding will automatically re-admit the indicator.
- **Alternative rejected:** redefining `pain_score_max` over all pre-index pain rows would also remove the alias, but at the cost of protocol fidelity (non-knee pain scores entering a knee pain predictor). Bookkeeping must not drive the predictor definition.
- **Status:** analyst decision, documented; affects a reported hazard ratio, so it is flagged for author review before the manuscript quotes the pain effect.

## D10. Riley candidate-parameter count is the IDENTIFIED count, not the column count (2026-07-24)
- **Original:** protocol section 16 requires the Riley minimum development sample to be computed "with the final number of candidate parameters".
- **Deviation:** P is taken from `identified_parameters` in `derived-data/cohort/m0_clinical_model.json`, computed by rank as `rank([X | 1]) - 1`, giving **P = 13** against 14 design columns. The previous calculation used P = 15.
- **Rationale:** Riley's criteria describe how many parameters the likelihood must estimate. Two directions in the design are not estimable: the aliased pain indicator (removed under D9) and patsy's `cr()` age basis, which is a **partition of unity** (`age_rcs1 + age_rcs2 + age_rcs3 = 1` on every row) and therefore spans the constant that an intercept-free Cox model cannot identify. A pairwise correlation scan cannot see a three-column relation, which is why identifiability is now decided by rank.
- **Impact:** the primary requirement falls from 1,502 to **1,301** patients. The previous P = 15 was **conservative** — it demanded more development data than the model needs — so every "development sample is sufficient" verdict already recorded still holds a fortiori. The age spline contributes 2 effective degrees of freedom, not 3, and no individual `age_rcs*` hazard ratio may be reported; the coefficient table flags them as level-unidentified.
- **Updated by D14/D15 (2026-07-24):** the M0 design changed again — `klg_contra_imp` and `klg_contra_missing` out, `days_to_index_imp` in — so M0 now has **13 design columns and P = 12 identified**, and the primary Riley requirement falls again from 1,301 to **1,201** patients. The method is unchanged; only the column set moved. Fewer parameters lower every requirement, so this direction is conservative with respect to the verdicts already recorded. Development n = 2,968 with 427 events remains **SUFFICIENT**.
- **Status:** analyst decision, documented.

## D12. Test-set precision: protocol floor met, analyst-adopted half-width target not met (2026-07-24)
- **Original:** protocol section 16 asks for "acceptable precision for 5-year AUROC and calibration slope" and states one numeric floor: "a practical preliminary floor is 500 total primary events and 100 test events". It specifies **no numeric precision target**.
- **Position:** the protocol floor **IS met** — 533 total primary events (>= 500) and 106 test events (>= 100). The +/-0.05 AUROC and +/-0.20 calibration-slope 95% CI half-widths in `config/feasibility.yaml` are **analyst-adopted** and are stricter than anything the protocol requires. A previous version of `outputs/sample_size.md` called them "the pre-specified targets" and juxtaposed the shortfall with a section-16 citation, which misattributed an analyst choice to the protocol; the config and the report now label them explicitly.
- **Finding:** by simulation, 741 test patients give a 5-year AUROC half-width of 0.065 and a calibration-slope half-width of 0.291 at an assumed true AUROC of 0.70. Meeting both adopted targets would need about 1,570 test patients. The simulation assumes a normal linear predictor and censoring independent of risk, so these are **lower bounds** on real-world uncertainty.
- **Proposed protocol amendment (not yet enacted; requires author decision before the test set is unsealed):** (a) re-specify the primary test-set estimand as a **paired** comparison against the clinical baseline M0 — a difference in AUROC and in the index of prediction accuracy — rather than an absolute AUROC level, since paired differences share patients and are far more precisely estimated; and (c) pre-specify the achievable precision honestly in the protocol and the manuscript, stating that the study is powered to demonstrate discrimination better than chance and better than the clinical baseline, not to certify a point estimate. Increasing the test fraction (option b) is explicitly **not** recommended: it requires re-drawing the LOCKED splits (protocol section 17), which is an investigator decision, and no module in this pipeline changes them.
- **Status:** flagged for author decision. Nothing has been re-split; the locked splits are untouched.

## D13. Protocol section 24 landmark sensitivity (day 30 / day 180) deferred (2026-07-24)
- **Original:** protocol section 24 lists "landmarks at day 30, day 90, and day 180" among the sensitivity analyses.
- **Deviation:** only the pre-specified primary landmark, **day 90**, is analysed. The day-30 and day-180 landmarks are **not run**; `outputs/tables/m0_metrics.csv` records the row `sens_landmark_30_90_180` as `not_run` with the reason.
- **Rationale:** the landmark is not an evaluation parameter that can be varied downstream; it defines the cohort. The module refuses to touch them.
- **What it would take:** add landmark-parameterised output paths so each alternative cohort is written to its own directory; re-derive splits per landmark under the same seed; re-run M0 on each; report the three side by side. Estimated as a self-contained task, not a variation of the current run.
- **Status:** deferred, documented. To be completed before submission or explicitly declared not done, with this rationale, in the manuscript's limitations.

## D14. Inferred KLG removed from M0; M1 added (CORRECTION, 2026-07-24)
- **Original:** protocol Table 7 defines the model ladder as **M0 = "Age, sex, comorbidities, pain, image-to-index interval"** and **M1 = "M0 plus inferred KLG"**. Protocol Table 6 lists the dataset-inferred contralateral KLG as a **"Secondary comparator only"**. Protocol Table 8 makes the primary estimand *M4 multimodal versus M0 clinical-only, IPCW cumulative/dynamic AUROC at 5 years*.
- **The defect:** an earlier revision of `config/feasibility.yaml` placed `klg_contra` in `features_clinical.primary_predictors`, so the fitted M0 design contained `klg_contra_imp` **and** `klg_contra_missing`. KLG was M0's **dominant coefficient** (HR 1.63, z 9.2; `klg_contra_missing` HR 2.06, z 3.2). The clinical comparator the image models must beat therefore already contained a radiograph-derived severity grade, which makes the primary estimand a comparison of imaging against imaging and biases the measured incremental value of imaging **toward zero**. Five tracked artefacts already stated that inferred KLG is a secondary comparator only (`outputs/protocol_deviations.md`, `outputs/data_quality_report.md`, `outputs/protocol_to_column_mapping.csv`, `outputs/feasibility_stage1_checkpoint.md`, `src/inventory.py`), so the fitted model contradicted the project's own record. **M1 did not exist anywhere** — no module, notebook or report defined it — leaving protocol Secondary objective 2 unimplemented.

Rank-based identifiability is computed and persisted for both designs. The test split stays sealed in both.

| M0 metric | before (KLG in M0) | after (protocol Table 7 M0) |
|---|---|---|
| design columns / identified parameters | 14 / 13 | **13 / 12** |
| chosen ridge penalizer | 0.01 | **1.0** (at the grid edge — see D23) |
| Harrell C-index (val) | 0.6677 | **0.6086** |

- **What inferred KLG actually adds** (M1, on the 359 KLG-eligible validation patients / 52 events, paired patient-level bootstrap, 500 replicates; both models refitted on the same eligible training rows so the *only* difference is the KLG column): Harrell C 0.5919 -> **0.6643**, difference **+0.0724 (95% CI -0.0015 to +0.1372, p = 0.064)**; IPCW AUROC at 1 y **+0.0819** (-0.016 to 0.167), at 2 y **+0.0618** (-0.034 to 0.135), at 5 y **+0.0300** (-0.050 to 0.100). - **Impact on the primary estimand:** M4-versus-M0 is now a comparison against routine clinical variables, as protocol Table 8 intends. M0's absolute performance is lower, which makes the imaging increment easier to demonstrate; that is not a reason to prefer this specification, it is a consequence of removing a predictor the protocol never put there. The manuscript must report M0's corrected numbers and must not quote the earlier ones.
- **Status:** correction, documented. No author sign-off sought: the protocol tables are unambiguous and the previous state contradicted them.

## D26. The 32-patient batch is assembled as 8 accumulation steps of 4, and the DataLoader runs 0 workers (2026-07-26)
- **Original:** `config/feasibility.yaml` sets `model_image.local.grad_accum_steps: 1` and `model_image.local.num_workers: 4`, so the 32-patient batch would be one forward/backward pass of 32 patients loaded by 4 spawned worker processes.
- **Deviation:** local training is invoked as `--grad-accum 8 --num-workers 0`, i.e. the same 32-patient optimisation batch assembled from **8 micro-batches of 4 patients**, with data loaded in the main process.
- **What did NOT change:** the optimisation batch is still 32 patients, the loss, optimizer, schedule, augmentation, seeds, horizons and the 512 x 512 crop are all untouched. **Nothing was downscaled.** Protocol section 13 fixes the crop at 512^2, and `notebooks/train_colab.ipynb` cell 34 states the preference for gradient accumulation over downscaling; this is that preference exercised.
- **Rationale (measured, not assumed — plan step A3):** ConvNeXt-Tiny holds roughly 0.5 GB of fp32 backward activations per 512^2 crop, and because batching is per *patient* while crops per patient vary (mean 1.64, max 5), a micro-batch of 8 patients can carry up to 23 crops, about 11.5 GB, on a 24 GB machine. It intermittently thrashes. Measured `m4_fusion` step times on this M4 Pro: accumulation 1 did not complete 23 steps in 11 minutes (> 25 s/step); accumulation 2, 47.8 s/step; accumulation 4 with 4 workers, 2.67 s/step; accumulation 4 with 0 workers, 1.91 s/step; **accumulation 8 with 0 workers, 0.50 s/step**; accumulation 16, 0.28 s/step but no better per epoch. Micro-batch 4 caps the worst case at 12 crops (about 6 GB) and sustains the machine's ceiling of roughly 13 crops/s.
- **Why 0 workers rather than 4:** `persistent_workers` is off and macOS spawns rather than forks, so the worker pool is rebuilt every epoch. That cost recurs; it does not amortise. Removing it saves 7 to 8 s per epoch, cuts the `m4_fusion` validation pass from 209 s to 10 s and the clinical epoch from 38 s to 16 s. Data loading is nowhere near the bottleneck at 13 crops/s. Augmentation still varies per epoch at 0 workers because `PatientViewDataset.__getitem__` XORs `self.epoch` into the per-sample RNG seed for exactly this case.
- **Why this is recorded rather than written into the config:** both values are inputs to `TrainSettings.training_contract()` and therefore to the training-contract hash, which is `d61f0d617a697fe4` under these flags (against `f781e47085e6c486` for the config defaults). `train_one_seed` **refuses** a checkpoint written under a different hash rather than silently resuming across a changed contract, so the flags are load-bearing and must be identical for every invocation of both stages. They are hardware-specific tuning, not a scientific choice, and the config keeps the machine-independent specification.
- **Honest note on cost:** the measured runtime is **worse than the plan's extrapolation**. Plan step A3 estimated 45 to 65 min per seed per image arm and 24 to 30 h for the full 7-arm ladder. The measurement gives 89 min per seed at 16 epochs and 223 min at 40, i.e. **Stage 1 15.5 to 38.7 h, Stage 2 15.4 to 38.4 h, full ladder 30.8 to 77.1 h** depending on whether early stopping bites near epoch 16 (best epoch about 8 plus patience 8) or the seeds run the full 40. `max_epochs: 40` and `patience: 8` were **not** reduced to save time: they are pre-specified in protocol sections 14 and 15, and shortening them after seeing a runtime measurement would be a post-hoc change to the training contract.
- **Status:** operational tuning, documented. No scientific parameter was altered and the test split was not read.

## D30. Net benefit estimated by Kaplan-Meier within the flagged set, not by per-patient IPCW (2026-07-30)
- **Original:** protocol section 18 names decision-curve analysis and cites Vickers but specifies **no estimator**. The textbook net-benefit formula assumes complete follow-up, which this cohort does not have: **473 of 741 test patients (63.8%) are censored before day 1825** and only 162 (21.9%) are followed past it. The naive event fraction, 14.3%, understates the 5-year risk of 20.0% by **5.7 percentage points**, which is exactly where the treat-all line crosses zero. The estimator is therefore not a detail; it decides where the reference line sits.
- **Choice:** at each threshold the flagged set's 5-year event risk is estimated by **Kaplan-Meier at day 1825 within that flagged set**, so the true-positive and false-positive terms come from one fit, no censored patient is dropped and none is counted twice. An **inverse-probability-of-censoring-weighted** curve is computed on the same flagged sets and reported beside it as `net_benefit_ipcw`, as a **point-estimate sensitivity analysis only**. It deliberately carries no interval: it shares the primary estimator's bootstrap draw, so a second band would invite a reader to compare two intervals that are not independent.
- **Measured, and this is why both curves are printed.** The **absolute level** of net benefit is estimator-dependent at this sample size. Over the plotted window the two estimators differ by up to **0.016 on test** (arm `m0` at p_t = 0.20: 0.0396 against 0.0557, i.e. the gap is **41% of that arm's own estimate**) and by up to **0.028 on validation** (arm `m2_frontal` at p_t = 0.26). No absolute net-benefit value in this paper should be read as if the estimator were settled.
- **Measured, and this is why the paper leans on contrasts.** The **differences between arms** are far more stable than the levels. Across the **90** threshold-by-contrast points at or below p_t = 0.30 (three arms against the `m0` reference, 30 thresholds each), the IPCW contrast falls inside the primary estimator's 95% bootstrap interval at **89 of 90**. The single exception is `m2_frontal` at p_t = 0.01, where the IPCW difference is +0.000279 against an interval of +0.000363 to +0.002267: outside by about 8e-05, at the lowest threshold on the grid, and of the same sign. And wherever the primary estimator's interval **excludes zero**, the IPCW contrast has the **same sign at every one of those points**. The manuscript's clinical-utility claims are therefore stated as contrasts, with the absolute curve presented as the shape of the argument rather than as a calibrated quantity.
- **Status:** analyst decision, documented. Both curves live in `outputs/tables/{split}_net_benefit.csv` and neither is derived from the other.

Withholding the lateral and sunrise views from the frozen M3 network costs **−0.078 AUROC (95% CI −0.142 to −0.012, P = .024)** on the 315 test patients who have more than one view, while withholding the sunrise view alone costs nothing detectable (+0.004 in M3, −0.001 in M4). Across the whole cohort a frontal-only model nevertheless matches the multi-view arms (0.837 vs 0.840 and 0.844), because 419 of 740 test patients (56.6%) have only a frontal film. The two results are different estimands and are reconciled in `outputs/manuscript/v6- resubmission/estimand_reconciliation.md`; **"additional views add nothing measurable" is not supported and must not be written.**
  - **Provenance constraint honoured.** Every Δ above is a within-run local float32 difference against a locally recomputed baseline, never against a published per-patient risk, exactly as the bullet above requires (D37). The local baseline hazards are bitwise identical to the Phase-0 recomputation, the local AUROC is 0.836692 against the published 0.836759, and case selection for the example panels was taken from the **published** risks, agreeing with the local assignment on 32 of 32 cases.
  - **Protocol section 22's own consequence clause — "a material change triggers artifact review" — is not triggered.** The largest masking-related change is −0.0154 AUROC at a band nearly three times the specified width. **One new artifact defect was nevertheless found and is recorded separately as D39**: the masked band is not exactly zero on 11.9% of test crops.
- **Status:** protocol-compliance gap, recorded in this register for the first time on 2026-08-10, executed and **reported at revision stage; the RESULTS block was populated on 2026-08-11 and this entry may now be cited as reported**. It stays **OPEN** on one item: the label-randomisation half of section 22 item 3 was not run and will not be, which is recorded as a scope cut above rather than as a completion. The gap in the original submission is a fact regardless of how the analyses turned out, and it stays in the register after them.

## D37. The artifact that reports the sealed-split estimates records no interpreter, device, platform or AMP setting (2026-08-10)
- **Original:** protocol section 28 and ordinary reproducibility practice require that the computational environment which produced a reported number be recorded with it. `derived-data/cohort/train_arms.json` does this properly: it carries an `interpreter` block giving python 3.12.13, `platform Linux-6.6.122+-x86_64-with-glibc2.35`, `torch 2.11.0+cu128`, `timm 1.0.28`, numpy 2.0.2, pandas 2.2.2, `device cuda`, `amp true`.
- **The defect.** `derived-data/cohort/test_scoring.json` — the artifact that records the single permitted read, the per-arm sealed-split negative log-likelihoods and the recalibration actually applied — carries the top-level keys `module`, `generated_utc`, `sealed_read`, `training_contract_hash`, `cohort`, `grid`, `ensemble`, `recalibration`, `arms`, **and nothing about the environment**. The strings `interpreter`, `device`, `platform`, `amp`, `torch`, `python`, `cuda` and `timm` do not occur anywhere in the file. **So the artifact that reports the headline out-of-sample estimates does not record that they were produced on a Google Colab GPU under float16 autocast.** That fact survives only in a different file, and only by inference: `train_arms.json` is stamped `2026-07-30T01:18:51Z` and `test_scoring.json` `2026-07-30T01:19:46Z`, 55 seconds apart in one session.
- **What it costs, measured rather than asserted.** A local float32 re-score of the same checkpoints over the same shard (`outputs/manuscript/v6- resubmission/phase0_gate_report.md`) gives:
  - **Aggregate discrimination and likelihood reproduce to ≤5.4e-04.** Largest AUROC deviation 5.39e-04, largest test-NLL deviation 9.12e-05. The primary 5-year AUROC reproduces to 7.9e-05 for `m4_fusion` and 6.7e-05 for `m2_frontal`.
  - **Per-patient predicted risks reproduce only to about 1e-02.** Maximum absolute difference in 5-year risk 9.7e-03 (`m4_fusion`) and 7.6e-03 (`m2_frontal`); at hazard level, 6.3e-03 and 5.2e-03, with **zero of 7,400 elements bit-exact**.
  - **The 5-year calibration slope is the least stable published statistic**: `m4_fusion` **published 1.023087, recomputed 1.017490**, a difference of **5.6e-03**. Both round to 1.02, which is the value the manuscript quotes, so the published number stands — but it should be treated as ±0.006, not ±0.0001.
  - Structural identity is **exact**: join keys identical and in identical order, shapes, interval edges, seed lists and every label array reproduce bit for bit, and the checkpoints load strictly under contract hash `4b862b5ecb947314`.
  - **The cause is established, not assumed.** A controlled experiment on one seed gives MPS-float32 versus CPU-float32 1.9e-06, and float16 versus float32 1.27e-02. The argument is a bound, not a derivation: precision alone moves hazards about twice as far on a single seed as the 6.3e-03 observed across a five-seed ensemble, and averaging can only shrink a perturbation, never enlarge it, so the precision regime accounts for the whole of the observed difference without needing any other cause. `config/feasibility.yaml` sets `amp_devices: ["cuda"]`, so a CUDA run is autocast and any local run is float32. Bit-exact reproduction on a machine without CUDA is impossible in principle. This must not be restated as a `1.27e-02 / √5 ≈ 5.7e-03` calculation: the two figures are maxima over populations of different size (640 elements against 7,400), maxima do not scale as `1/√n`, and five seeds' float16 perturbations are not independent. Section 5.1 of `outputs/manuscript/v6- resubmission/phase0_gate_report.md` sets this out in full, including the apples-to-apples mean-difference ratio of 1.71.
- **What this does and does not invalidate.** Nothing is wrong with the published estimates: they are what the frozen models produce in the environment that produced them, and that environment is recorded one file away. What is missing is the record in the artifact a reader would check. The gap is one of provenance, not of arithmetic.
- **It cannot be repaired, and that is the reason it is a register entry rather than a bug fix.** `test_scoring.json` is the frozen record of the sealed read. Regenerating it would mean re-running `src/score_test.py` over the locked split, which is the second scored read protocol sections 12 and 17 forbid and D31 says is spent. **The gap is therefore permanent in the artifact and is closed only by disclosure** — this entry, plus a Methods sentence.
- **What it obliges the revision to do.** State the training and scoring environment in Methods from `train_arms.json.interpreter`, not from `outputs/environment_training.json` (**D38**). Quote aggregate metrics to three decimals and per-patient predicted risks to no more than two. Never re-derive the 5-year calibration slope locally and present it as the published value. For any newly computed per-patient quantity, say which run produced it (see D36, where every occlusion and masking delta must be a within-run difference).
- **Status:** reproducibility gap, documented. Found independently by two agents during Phase 0 of the v6 revision and recorded in `phase0_gate_report.md` section 7.1.

## D38. `outputs/environment_training.json` describes the local Mac venv, not the environment that produced the models (2026-08-10)
- **Original:** protocol section 28 asks for the computational environment of record. `outputs/environment_training.json` is the only file in the aggregate outputs tree that names itself for that role.
- **The defect is not what the file says, it is what it will be taken to mean.** The file is internally honest — its own `purpose` field reads "Phase 2 **local** training environment (protocol section 28)" — and it records `venv /Users/samersalman/.venvs/mrkr-torch`, `platform macOS-26.5.1-arm64-arm-64bit`, `torch 2.13.0`, `cuda_available false`, `mps_available true`, `default_dtype torch.float32`, written at `2026-07-26T16:57:05+00:00`. **2026-07-26 is the day D27 records that image-model training moved off this machine to a Google Colab GPU.** So the file describes the machine on which the reported ladder was **not** trained, three days before the ladder that was reported existed.
- **Why this is more than a stale timestamp.** `derived-data/` is git-ignored, so `train_arms.json` — the file that actually records the training and scoring environment — **is not in the public code release**, while `outputs/environment_training.json` is. A reviewer who follows the archived-code link looking for the training environment finds the wrong one, and it is the only one there.
- **What is true.** Every reported model was trained and scored on `Linux-6.6.122+-x86_64-with-glibc2.35`, python 3.12.13, `torch 2.11.0+cu128`, `timm 1.0.28`, numpy 2.0.2, pandas 2.2.2, `device cuda`, `amp true`. The pins in `requirements-training.txt` (`torch==2.13.0`, `torchvision==0.28.0`, `timm==1.0.28`) describe the **local re-run** environment, not the training one, and differ from the training torch by a minor version. All three records are legitimate records of different things; **none of them is "the training environment" on its own**, and only `train_arms.json.interpreter` is the training environment.
- **Consequence.** Any sentence in the manuscript, in the CLAIM or TRIPOD+AI checklists, or in either README that presents `environment_training.json` as the environment that produced the models is **incorrect and must be corrected**. The v6 Methods must cite `train_arms.json.interpreter` for training and scoring, and may cite `environment_training.json` only as the local analysis and re-run environment, labelled as such. This entry does not edit the file: a record of a real local environment is worth keeping, and rewriting it would destroy the evidence that the local path was attempted and abandoned (D26, D27).
- **Why this is a separate entry from D37, since the two share a cause.** They are different failure modes with different remedies and different closure conditions. **D37 is an omission in a frozen artifact that cannot be repaired without a second sealed read**, so it can only ever be closed by disclosure. **D38 is a mis-description in a live, published output file that any document can cite and probably will**, and it is closed by fixing the citing documents. Merging them into one entry would make it impossible to close either half independently, would bury the fact that only one of the two is repairable, and would let a reader conclude from a single "environment" heading that one disclosure sentence discharges both. The measured numeric consequence they share is stated once, in D37, and cross-referenced from here rather than duplicated.
- **Status:** reproducibility gap, documented. Found independently by two agents during Phase 0 of the v6 revision and recorded in `phase0_gate_report.md` section 7.2.

## D39. The masked border band is not exactly zero on 11.9% of test crops, because marker masking runs after border masking (2026-08-11)
- **Original:** protocol **section 13** specifies a fixed masked border band on every prepared crop — 6% of each edge, 31 px of a 512 px crop, 22.75% of the image area — and the pipeline treats "the band is zero" as a contract. `src/preprocess_images.py` asserts it (`assert_border_is_zero`), `outputs/crop_qa_checklist.md` reports `masked_pct` against it, and every downstream statement about burned-in laterality letters and technique text rests on the band having been removed rather than merely overwritten.
- **The defect.** The band is **not exactly zero on 145 of the 1,216 test crops (11.9%)** — by view, frontal **6.8%**, lateral **25.2%**, sunrise **16.2%**. **Cause:** `preprocess_images.crop_stages` runs `mask_borders` **first** and `mask_burned_in_markers` **second**. The marker masker does not blank a marker; it fills a *dilated* region (`marker_merge_px` = 10 px) with the **median of the surrounding ring**, and where a marker sits just inboard of the band that dilated fill writes a non-zero value back **into** the band. Maximum value observed **48/255** (the ring median is capped at `marker_ring_max_level` = 60). It affects **0.049%** of all border-band pixels. Measured over the whole test split and recorded in `outputs/tables/interp_regions.csv` (`n_crops_with_nonzero_border_band`, `border_band_nonzero_pixel_share`, `border_band_max_value`, and the three per-view rates).
- **Why the assertion did not catch it.** `assert_border_is_zero` is called from `TrainingData.__init__`, which probes **exactly one training image**. A per-image or per-shard check would have failed on the first affected lateral crop. The guard was written as a smoke test and read as a contract.
- **What it costs, measured rather than asserted: essentially nothing.** The section-22 occlusion experiment prices it directly. Re-zeroing the band on every test crop moves discrimination by **Δ AUROC −0.0002 (95% CI −0.0009 to 0.0000)**, and blanking every residual marker-like blob in the image moves it by **−0.0010 (95% CI −0.0023 to −0.0001)** (`outputs/tables/interp_occlusion.csv`, arm `m2_frontal`, n = 734, 106 events, 2,000 paired bootstrap replicates). Grad-CAM attribution mass in the band is also indistinguishable between affected and unaffected crops — 0.074 on the 58 frontal crops carrying a footprint against 0.084 on the 800 that do not — so the footprints do not attract attribution either. **The models were trained and scored on these crops and no reported estimate changes.**
- **Why it is a register entry rather than a bug fix.** Repairing the ordering means re-running preprocessing, which would rewrite the shards the frozen ladder was trained on and the test shard the single permitted read scored (D31, D35). The staged DICOM tree no longer exists on this machine in any case (see D22 and the note at the top of `outputs/crop_qa_checklist.md`). **The crops of record cannot be regenerated, so the contract violation is permanent in the artifacts and is closed only by disclosure.** For any future run the fix is one line — mask borders **after** masking markers, or re-apply the border mask as the last stage — and the guard should probe every image rather than one.
- **What it obliges the revision to do.** Do not write that the border band "is zeroed" without qualification. Write that it is zeroed and then, on 11.9% of test crops, partially overwritten by the marker masker with values up to 48/255 over 0.049% of band pixels, at a measured cost of Δ AUROC −0.0002. **It also disqualifies one sentence:** "blanking the band left 677 of 734 patients' predicted risk unchanged to the last bit" is a statement about how few crops carry a non-zero band, **not** evidence about burned-in text, and neither is the border-only negative control (see D36 RESULTS, where the circularity is set out).
- **Discovery.** Found on 2026-08-10 by the protocol section-22 interpretability work (`_handoff/T6.md` §3), which flagged it as "new, unnumbered". It is numbered here.
- **Status:** contract violation in a frozen artifact, documented, permanent, and analytically priced. No estimate is affected. Also recorded in `outputs/crop_qa_checklist.md` §2.

## D40. Protocol section 23 (i): the 400-image review is declined by author decision, not deferred (2026-08-12)
- **Original:** protocol section 23 (i) requires two independent reviewers with orthopaedic or musculoskeletal imaging experience to score a stratified random sample of at least **400 index images** on laterality, view, native-knee status, crop adequacy, burned-in text and gross non-knee content; to report **raw agreement and Cohen kappa per item**; to resolve disagreements by consensus or a third reviewer; and to **expand the review if any critical-error rate exceeds 2%**. It is also the academic editor's **first "Essential" item** on the v6 revision list. **D22** is the entry that has carried it as outstanding since 2026-07-24.
- **Deviation: the review will not be performed.** The author has decided that a 400-image, two-reviewer, six-item review — roughly 4,800 independent judgements before adjudication, and 8 to 11 person-hours of two qualified readers' time — is **disproportionate** to what it can establish on this cohort, and has declined it. This is a decision, not a deferral. No reviewer will be sought, no schedule exists, the sampling material has been withdrawn (see the 2026-08-12 update in D22), and no document in the package may describe the review as outstanding, planned, in progress or imminent.
- **What it would have measured, stated so the loss is legible:** per-item raw agreement and Cohen kappa between two independent readers over 400 sampled crops; a per-item critical-error rate against the pre-specified 2% gate, with an obligation to expand the review if any item failed it; and, on the 318 development panels that carry a full film beside the crop, direct visual confirmation that the pipeline kept the **contralateral** half.
- **What its absence costs, itemised rather than summarised.**
  1. **No human has inspected any image the reported results rest on.** The 82 test-split rows of this sample were the only route to that, and they are withdrawn with the rest. Every test-split number in the manuscript rests on automated crop statistics alone.
  2. **No agreement statistic and no Cohen kappa exists for either protocol section 23 review** — (i) is declined and (ii) is unadjudicated — so the study reports no measure of human reader agreement on anything.
  3. **The 2% critical-error gate is never evaluated.** The study cannot say that crop quality clears the threshold it set for itself; it can only say the threshold was not tested. That is a weaker statement than "it passed" and must not be written as one.
  4. **The five crop-decidable items go unverified on every split**, not only on the sealed one: view, native-knee status, crop adequacy, burned-in text and non-knee content were assessable on all 400 rows and none is now assessed.
- **What its absence does NOT cost, because on one item the loss is smaller than it looks.** Half-selection was **already** unreachable on the reported split before this decision: the source DICOMs are gone, the 82 test panels are crop-only, and a finished crop is anatomically identical whichever half was taken, because every pre-index film shows two native knees, `standardize_to_left` mirrors right knees away and the border mask removes any burned-in side marker (**D22**, 2026-08-10). Declining the review therefore removes no evidence about **test-split** laterality that would otherwise have existed. The laterality evidence that does exist is the **protocol section 7 audit, which is complete**: 98.0% agreement with the recovery rule on 200 development patients, recorded in the 2026-08-12 update inside D22.
- **Why this is a register entry rather than a silence.** It drops a protocol requirement and an editor's Essential item by choice. A requirement abandoned by decision has to be visible **as a decision** — in this register, in the manuscript's limitations and in the response letter — or the record reads as work still in the queue. The project has been caught reporting an unchanged status line while the underlying state moved; this entry exists so that does not happen again in the other direction.
- **Reporting consequences, which are bounded and specific.** **CLAIM item 18** (inter- and intrarater variability) and **TRIPOD+AI item 7** (data pre-processing and quality checking) stay **No**. Their **status does not change**; their stated **ground** does, from "not yet scored" to "declined", because neither item can now become Yes by waiting. `outputs/crop_qa_checklist.md` sections 1, 3 and 6 are rewritten to the same effect: the sign-off block stays empty, the four reviewer criteria stay unticked, and the outstanding-work table records the decline rather than a queue.
- **Status:** author decision, taken and implemented. Protocol section 23 (i) is **closed unmet**. D22 stays open on the section 23 (ii) outcome-record audit alone.
